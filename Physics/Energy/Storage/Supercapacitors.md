Electric Double Layer
 
- Forms on surface of charged object when placed into liquid
- Two parallel layers of charge
	- Surface charge
	- Other in flued
		- Screens the first layer
- Most apparent in systems with a large ratio of surface area to volume, such as colloids or porous materials with particles or pores on the scale of microns or even nanometres

Electrochemical Supercapacitors
 
- Reversible adsorption of ions from electrolyte onto porous electrodes to form EDL
	- At electrode/electrolyte interface
- Electric double-layer capacitors
	- EDLC
	- Ultracapacitor
	- Strictly only involving physical adsorption without chemical reactions
	- Each electrode-electrolyte interface represents a capacitor
		- Complete cell comprises two in series
- Pseudo-capacitor
	- Uses chemical reactions
		- Charge transfer
		- Oxidation/reduction

Construction
 
- High surface area
- Capacitance
	- Nanoporous activated carbon
		- The surface area of activated carbon is typically \>1000 m2/g which results in specific capacitance values \> 105F/kg
- Voltage
	- Electrolyte stability
	- Aqueous electrolytes offer advantages in higher ionic conductivity
		- Low breakdown potential
			- 1.2 V
	- Organic electrolytes allow operating up to 3 V
		- Order of magnitude higher resistance
	- Ionic liquids are new
		- No solvent
			- Just liquid salts
		- Room-temperature inorganic liquid salts
		- Up to 4 V
- No chemical reactions occur if applied voltage less than electrolyte breakdown threshold

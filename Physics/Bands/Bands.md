Holes
 
Holes are positive-mass positive-charged because electrons that would exist have negative effective-mass
 
- _The_ _dispersion relation_ _determines how electrons respond to forces (via the concept of_ _effective mass__)_
- _Electrons near the top of the_ _valence band_ _behave as if they have_ _negative mass_
	- Solely due to shape of band
- _Positively-charged holes as a shortcut for calculating the total current of an almost-full band_
	- Nearly full band
		- No current
			- When almost full, take off current from electrons that would be where holes are
				- Taking off a negative current = adding a positive current
- _A hole near the top of the valence band moves the same way as an electron near the top of the valence band_ **would** _move_[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_hole#cite_note-Kittel-4)
	- Which is in the opposite direction compared to conduction-band electrons experiencing the same force.
	- Because they would have negative mass
- _Conclusion: Hole is a positive-charge, positive-mass_ _quasiparticle_ \> From \<[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_hole)\>  
 \> From \<[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_hole)\>

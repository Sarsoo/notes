Fermi-Dirac Distribution
 $$f \left(E\right) = \frac{1}{e^{\left(\frac{E - E_{f}}{k T}\right)} + 1}$$  
![Full 0.8 0.2 Ernpty Tl T2 Energy eV](../../img/OneNote/Fermi%20image%20a25b1d3cd0035d6c.png)  

- At $T = 0$
	- All states below Fermi level are full
	- All above are empty
- Raising temperature smears across this line
 ![The probability that a particle will have energy E...](../../img/OneNote/Fermi%20image%20abb54cb6feffd0e6.png)

- Without +1 is the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution
- + 1 for fermions
	- Pain for maths
- Ignore + 1 generally
	- Valid when Ec - Ef \> 3kT
		- $f \left(E\right) = e^{- \left(\frac{E - E_{F}}{k T}\right)}$
			- Used for electrons
		- Fermi level not too close to the conduction band edge
	- Ev - Ef \< -3kT
		- $f \left(E\right) = 1 - e^{- \left(\frac{E - E_{F}}{k T}\right)}$
			- Used for holes
	- Non-degenerate cases
	- Must use full F-D when
		- Fermi close to bands
		- Or low temperature
		- Degenerate semiconductors

Energy
 
_Energy difference between highest and lowest occupied single-particle states at absolute-zero_
 
_Since the Fermi level in a metal at absolute zero is the energy of the highest occupied single particle state, then the Fermi energy in a metal is the energy difference between the Fermi level and lowest occupied single-particle state, at zero-temperature_

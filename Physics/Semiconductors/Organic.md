## Quartz Crystal Microbalance

QCM

- Measure thickness during deposition
- Use piezoelectric substrate
	- AC voltage causes resonance
	- Resonant frequency is related to mass
	- Slight changes in mass can be measured
		- Resonant frequency changed
	- Thickness as low as 0.1 nm
	- Need to know density
 
## Profilometry

- Measure thickness
- Scan needle across surface
- Advantages
	- Quick, simple
	- Wide thickness range
	- Fairly cheap
- Disadvantages
	- Slightly damaging to organic films
		- Makes physical contact
	- Only after deposition

## Atomic Force Microscope

AFM

- 0.1-0.2 nm vertical resolution
- Tip on flexible cantilever
	- Atomic forces (VDW) deflect tip
	- Measure deflection of laser incident on cantilever
- Contact Mode
	- Constant force between tip and surface
- Non-contact Mode
	- Cantilever oscillates
	- Oscillation frequency changed by atomic force
		- Compared to reference frequency
	- More sensitive
 ![detector laser cantilever tip](../../img/OneNote/Organic%20image%20b0cfb646c6825ff3.png)  

## Scanning Electron Microscopy

- Lots of information, very useful
- Allows measurement of
	- Topography
	- Morphology
	- Composition
	- Crystallographic Information
- Sample must be conductive
	- Or coated with thin conductive layer
- Fairly large excitation volume
 ![Electron beam C 1 Secondary electrons low E Light ...](../../img/OneNote/Organic%20image%2024a105a4e4c2fcd3.png)  

## Scanning Tunnelling Microscope

- 0.1 nm
	- Crazy good resolution
- Probes information on spatial variation in electronic DOS
- Sample must be atomically flat
	- Use materials that cleave to give flat surfaces
	- Conductive
- Slow
- Constant Current
	- Change height to maintain current
	- Profile as a function of height
- Constant Height
	- Profile as a function of current

## X-Ray Diffraction Imaging

XRD

- Structure of sample causes diffraction in incident x-rays
- Measure angles and intensities of diffraction
	- Can create 3D picture of density of electrons in crystal
	- Allows measurement of
		- Positions of atoms
		- Chemical Bonds
		- Disorder
- Bragg diffraction
 $2 d s i n ⁡ 𝜃 = n \lambda$  

- $d$ is spacing
- $𝜃$ is scattering angle
- $\lambda$ is wavelength
- $n$ is diffraction order

The phrase **Fourth Industrial Revolution** was first introduced by [Klaus Schwab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Schwab), executive chairman of the [World Economic Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Forum), in a 2015 article published by _Foreign Affairs_,[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Industrial_Revolution#cite_note-2) "Mastering the Fourth Industrial Revolution" was the 2016 theme of the [World Economic Forum Annual Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Forum#Annual_meeting_in_Davos), in Davos-Klosters, Switzerland.

- Interconnection
	- Ability of machines, devices, sensors, and people to connect and communicate with each other via the Internet of things, or the internet of people (IoP)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Industrial_Revolution#cite_note-auto-23)
- Information transparency
	- Transparency afforded by Industry 4.0 technology provides operators with comprehensive information to inform decisions. Inter-connectivity allows operators to collect immense amounts of data and information from all points in the manufacturing process, identify key areas that can benefit from improvement to increase functionality[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Industrial_Revolution#cite_note-auto-23)
- Technical assistance
	- Technological facility of systems to assist humans in decision-making and problem-solving, and the ability to help humans with difficult or unsafe tasks[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Industrial_Revolution#cite_note-24)
- Decentralized decisions
	- Ability of cyber physical systems to make decisions on their own and to perform their tasks as autonomously as possible. Only in the case of exceptions, interference, or conflicting goals, are tasks delegated to a higher level[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Industrial_Revolution#cite_note-25)

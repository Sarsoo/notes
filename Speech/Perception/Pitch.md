Place Theory
 
- Reference position along basilar membrane where stimulation occurs
- Linear distance measured from apex to max stimulation is proportional to log of input frequency
 
1. Locate $f_{0}$ component
2. Minimum frequency deltas between harmonics
	- (n+1)*f0-n*f0=f0
3. Highest common factor of frequency components
   

# Methods

1. Pitch corresponds to f0 of sound
	- Can identify pitch without f0
2. Adjacent harmonics, when present, allow inference of f0
	- ![Machine generated alternative text 210 210 210 Lin...](../../../img/OneNote/Pitch%20image%2058e483af1dfb36d8.png)
	- Identifies pitch even with weird spacing
3. Pitch identified from highest common factor of present harmonics
	- Handles exceptions from 1 and 2
	- Residue/virtual pitch/pitch of residue
		- Adjacent harmonics
 
# Limitations

- Discrimination of frequency difference in pitch
- Cannot resolve harmonics above about 5 - 7 to $f_{0}$ because critical bandwidth is too wide
- Pitch of non-harmonic sound
	- Ss higher than sh
		- Sh has higher energy in the lower end
- Pitch below 50 Hz

Temporal Theory
 
- Analyse frequency of nerve stimulations
- Stereocilia wave back and forth with sound waves
	- Only accept ions when bending in one direction
	- Fire at particular time or phase in each cycle of wave
		- Phase locking
		- In sync with sound frequency
	- Periodic
- Frequencies coded by which neurons (place) and how often
- Up to about 5 kHz
	- Phase locking breaks down after

[(British Psychological Society) Hearing pitch – right place, wrong time?](https://thepsychologist.bps.org.uk/volume-25/edition-12/hearing-pitch-%E2%80%93-right-place-wrong-time)
 
_An intriguing aspect of pitch perception is that the overall repetition rate of a tone, and its perceived pitch, is unaffected by removing the first harmonic, or ‘fundamental’ component, that has a frequency equal to the repetition rate of the tone. For example, a tone with harmonics of 200, 300, and 400 Hz (the second, third and fourth harmonic of a 100 Hz repetition rate) is heard as having a pitch corresponding to 100 Hz, even though there is no harmonic present at 100 Hz. Similarly, if harmonics of 600, 800, and 1000 Hz (the third, fourth, and fifth harmonic of a 200 Hz repetition rate) are present, a pitch corresponding to 200 Hz is heard._

Subjective
 
- Not measured directly
- Usually referred to by the frequency of the pure tone that sounds the same
- Or fundamental frequency of complex tone
- Somewhat affected by sound level
	- Below 2 kHz
		- Decrease with higher volume
	- Above 4 kHz
		- Increase with higher volume

Just-noticeable Difference
 
- Limen
	- Sensory threshold
	- Liminal
		- Barely perceptible
- DLF
	- Which tone has higher pitch
	- Change smallest at low frequencies
	- Increases monotonically with frequency
	- As proportion of centre frequency
		- Small at middle frequencies
		- Larger at very high and very low
- FMDL
	- Use frequency modulated tones
	- Varies less with frequency than DLF
	- 2-4 Hz
- Both get smaller as sound level increases
